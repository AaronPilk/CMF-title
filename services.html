Commercial Title Insurance

Bigger deals, longer exception lists

Commercial files carry bigger numbers and messier histories — easements, tenant estoppels, survey conflicts, entity chains that take real work to trace.

The coverage should match what's actually at stake. We build the policy to the deal rather than to a template.

Effectively all of them. It matters most on commercial deals, where the prices are higher and the agreements more complicated — but a residential buyer with a clouded title has the same problem on a smaller scale.

Anything that surfaces later and challenges your ownership — a recording error, a lien nobody released, a forged signature, an heir nobody knew about.

Without a policy, defending that claim and any loss on it comes out of your pocket.

The lender's policy protects the lender for the loan amount. The owner's policy protects you for the purchase price. If you're financing, you'll typically be buying both.

The lender's policy shrinks as the loan is paid down. The owner's doesn't — it holds for as long as you have an interest in the property.
